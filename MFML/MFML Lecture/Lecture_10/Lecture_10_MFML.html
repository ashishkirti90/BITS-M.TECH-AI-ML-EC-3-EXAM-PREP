Valley Problem

Unscaled features create elliptical valleys (Slides 16–21: differential curvature). Gradients don't point to the minimum, causing oscillation. $$ L = w_1^2 + \alpha w_2^2 $$ $\alpha=1$: perfect circular bowl ($x^2+y^2$) — one big step lands on the optimum. $\alpha=4$: the lecture's elliptical bowl ($x^2+4y^2$).
On run, the gold arrow shows $-\nabla L$ at the start: always ⊥ to the contour line (Slide 19), but pointing at the optimum (dotted line) only when contours are circles. Steepest descent is just the instantaneous best direction.
Watch the contours turn from ellipses to circles. This is what Standardization (Z-score) does.

Local Optima (Slides 11–15)

The lecture's exact function: $$ F(x) = (x-1)^2\big((x-3)^2-1\big) $$ Critical points: $x=1$ (local min, $F=0$), $x=1.634$ (max, $F=0.348$), $x=3.366$ (global min, $F=-4.848$).
Start anywhere left of $x=1.634$ and GD can only reach the local minimum — it never learns a better one exists.
$F = A_1(x_1)+\dots+A_d(x_d)$, each with $k$ local minima $\Rightarrow k^d$ minima total (Slides 13–14).
59,049 local minima to get stuck in
Initialisation (Slide 3): draw components from $[-1,+1]$; use $[0,1]$ if parameters must be non-negative. Correlated components and badly scaled magnitudes can cause overflow / underflow during updates — the starting point can be critical.

Momentum Physics

Slides 14–15: flat regions (gradient ≈ 0) make descent painfully slow, and local minima trap it entirely. Momentum adds "mass" to the optimizer, letting it coast across flat stretches and roll through shallow local minima.
