A linear model’s loss is a convex bowl — one minimum, gradient always points home. Every nonlinear unit adds a wave. Write the loss as F(w₁,w₂) = A₁(w₁) + A₂(w₂): if A₁ has k₁ wells and A₂ has k₂, the surface has k₁ × k₂ minima — in d dimensions, ∏ kᵢ. Depth multiplies wells, the count explodes, and GD can park in any of them — possibly far from the global one.

WHY SCALING WORKS — THE CHAIN OF LOGIC
1 · UNEQUAL SENSITIVITY

Raw features live on wildly different ranges (Age 22–60 vs Experience 0–50 — the lecture’s wealth-vs-education example). The loss becomes far more sensitive to one weight than the other: differential curvature, κ = λmax/λmin ≫ 1 — and −∇L stops pointing at the optimum (it only points ⊥ to the contour you stand on).

2 · OSCILLATE, CRAWL, TRAP

One step size must fit the steepest direction, so the agent overshoots and zig-zags across the valley while barely crawling along it. In that near-flat direction the bowl’s pull ≈ 0 — even a shallow NN dent out-pulls it. The agent parks in a local minimum, far from the star.

3 · SIMILAR VARIANCE ⇒ ROUND BOWL

Mean-center and divide by σ (z-score), or min-max to [0,1]: features get similar variance, curvature becomes moderate in every direction, −∇L aims at ★, larger steps are safe, and momentum hops straight through the dents. Same bumps — different destiny.
