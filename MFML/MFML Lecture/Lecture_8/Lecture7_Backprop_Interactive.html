Backpropagation is just the chain rule, applied to a function built by composing many small functions, organized so that no piece of arithmetic is ever repeated.
forward signal
values flowing left → right (x, Z, a, loss)
backward blame
gradients flowing right → left (δ, every ∂Loss/∂·)
parameters
the knobs training adjusts (A, b)

1 · The computation graph, step by step (slides 11–14)

In everyday words Give a name to every small step of the scary function, compute the steps one at a time writing each value down, then walk backwards through them, reusing the numbers you already wrote. The nightmare formula never appears.

The function: f(x) = √(x² + ex²) + cos(x² + ex²), broken into a=x², b=eᵃ, c=a+b, d=√c, e=cos c, f=d+e. Drag x, then click Step to watch the forward pass fill values left-to-right, and the backward pass carry blame right-to-left. Blame at a node = sum over outgoing arrows of (local rate) × (blame at the arrow’s head).

2 · The sigmoid valve (slides 17–20)

In everyday words Each sigmoid neuron is a valve on the blame pipe. An undecided neuron (output near 0.5) is wide open — blame flows through. A confident, saturated neuron (output near 0 or 1) is almost shut — blame is blocked. The valve’s opening is the slope a(1−a), free to compute once you have a.
a = σ(Z) = 0.500000 valve opening a(1−a) = 0.250000

Blame through this one valve

blame in 1.000 → × 0.250 → blame out 0.250
δ(Z) = δ(a) · a(1−a) — rule (5), one wire

Why deep sigmoid nets fade: blame after k layers (best case)

even wide-open valves multiply blame by ≤ 0.25 — the seed of the “vanishing gradient” problem

3 · One full layer, forward and backward (slides 16–25 — the exact numbers from the study guide)

In everyday words A layer mixes its input, then squashes it. Backward, you undo in reverse: un-squash (scale by valve openings), then un-mix (send blame back along the wires). On the way past, read the blame on the layer’s own knobs. Hover over the ∂Loss/∂A grid at step 6 to see blame × signal literally.
x→ Ax+b→ Z→ σ→ a→ Loss
