The setup

One function, one gradient, one rule.

Our landscape is the simplest bowl there is — a paraboloid that dips to zero at the origin and climbs in every direction. Its gradient (the compass pointing steepest uphill) is just twice the position vector:

$$f(x,y)=x^2+y^2,\qquad \nabla f=\Big[\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x},\ \tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}\Big]=[\,2x,\ 2y\,].$$

So at the starting point $(2,3)$ the gradient is $[\,2(2),\,2(3)\,]=[\,4,\,6\,]$ — it points away from the centre, with magnitude $\sqrt{4^2+6^2}=\sqrt{52}\approx 7.21$. To descend, we step the opposite way, scaled by a learning rate $\eta$:

$$\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}\ \leftarrow\ \begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}-\eta\,\nabla f=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}-\eta\begin{bmatrix}2x\\2y\end{bmatrix}=(1-2\eta)\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}.$$

That last form is a gift: each step just multiplies your position by $(1-2\eta)$. You can literally predict the behaviour from that one number — and the demo lets you feel it.

The bowl itself — the loss surface

The same function as a 3D surface, $z=x^2+y^2$. The red ball sits on the surface above your chosen point; the gold trail is the descent path so far. Drag to rotate the view. Everything stays in sync with the panel above.

Tip: the ball glides to the bottom of the bowl exactly when the point above reaches the centre.

What to try

The learning rate is the whole story.

Because every step multiplies your position by $(1-2\eta)$, you can predict the fate of the descent before you press Run. Set the point back to $(2,3)$ and try each regime:

ηfactor (1−2η)what you'll see
0.100.80Smooth, steady glide into the centre. The textbook case.
0.500.00One single step lands exactly on the minimum. Perfectly tuned.
0.80−0.60Overshoots and zig-zags through the centre, spiralling in.
1.00−1.00Bounces between two points forever — never converges, never diverges.
> 1.00|factor| > 1Each step lands farther out. The path explodes off the bowl.
The teaching moment

Watch the gold gradient vector as the ball nears the centre: it shrinks toward zero. That's the signal that you've arrived — flat ground, $\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$, nowhere left to step. In a real model, that's the moment training stops making progress.
